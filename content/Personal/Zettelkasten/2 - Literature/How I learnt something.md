# Part 1
##  Classrooms suck at making you learn something. 

Proof: If you've ever interacted me or if you've read enough of this website, you might know that I am descent with English and I also have a descent accent. The point is not I am  good at something, the point is, **how did I acquire it and how you can too.** 

See, 90% of the English that i know is not because I learnt it in school or my peers talked to me in English. **It is because I played video games!** Since, my childhood, I've been playing storytelling games like the Call of Duty Black Ops series, the Resident Evil series, the Hitman series and many others.

The difference between School and these games was simple.
### School made me memorize while the Game characters "just spoke in English"

Just listening to game characters and interpreting it was wayyy better than those shitty school teacher who taught English by making us memorize stuff and merely translating to Hindi. If all i need was a translator, google is better and more accurate than my teacher.  

# Part 2
Whenever you want to learn something, just get straight to doing it. 

Like when I was first introduced to Javascript to create a website for Scrapyard Vidisha, I didn't know shit about Javascript. Heck, I didn't even know how to declare a variable back then. All I knew was how to do some basic math with Python. 

That said, I just tinkered with it and got myself familiar with it.

## Why not tutorials?
See, when you follow a tutorial, you are presented  "The Perfect Way" to do that and you miss all of the learning behind finding that solution. 

Another reason is that you will *feel* that you're consuming knowledge but actually you're just being overloaded with information that you won't be able to sustain that info.

You'll miss out on **why** something is that way and you won't appreciate it until you've gone the wrong way and failed.

## The Solution
Just take some existing broiler plate/template and start tweaking it. Once you're a bit comfy with the syntax (you can do basic math in that language), jump straight into the Docs or language specific books like The Rust Book.
